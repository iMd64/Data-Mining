Anyone can make information

Statisticians, mathematicians, anyone who finished high school can analyze a data set. Take a bank like İş Bankası with 15 million customers. It might report that 60% are male, 70% live in big cities such as İzmir, İstanbul or Ankara, under 5% live in the east of Turkey, and 3% are very rich.

Floor three: knowledge

Knowledge is different. You reach it with hard mathematical calculations, deeper statistical analysis and data science algorithms run over a bank’s or a GSM company’s data sets. The higher you go, the wiser you are and the more benefit you gain. Decisions made from knowledge are wisdom.

00:52:40to 00:53:42Slide 11

Correlation, or association

Technically this is called association. As Slide 11 stresses, a relationship does not automatically imply causation. In data mining, we uncover transaction co-occurrences without assuming one caused the other. Later in the semester, this is formalized through Association Rule Mining (using support, confidence, and lift metrics) to govern shelf merchandising and automated recommendation engines.

00:53:42to 00:57:03Slide 4 & 13

The whole journey

Recap of the pyramid: data at the first layer, information at the second, knowledge at the third and wisdom at the top. Wisdom is you becoming an expert and making final decisions as a human being, for future prediction.

Data science is this journey from data to information, from information to knowledge, from knowledge to wisdom. Using data mining algorithms and statistics over data sets gives knowledge, and as decision makers we then decide. This is the bird’s-eye view of the lesson. 00:58:42to 01:02:13Slide 3

Why it is called mining

Mining is madencilik in Turkish. In a mountain with copper, alloys, silver and iron you dig out big rocks, collect them and refine them to get the metals. Centuries ago it meant coal, and industry one ran on steam power from coal.

Now we mine data. Slide 3 delivers the foundational maxim: “Mining does not create the treasure — it discovers what is already hidden in the data.” Data mining is not collecting more data; it is discovering useful, previously unknown patterns, groups, trends, and anomalies hidden within large repositories. Tons of sand may yield a few grams of gold, and that small nugget is immensely valuable. We dig data to extract very valuable results.

01:02:13to 01:04:14Slide 10

Scale and careers

A GSM company such as Turkcell has more than 40 million customers, and a bank such as Yapı Kredi more than 20 million. They hold many data points on each: gender, birth year, home address, salary. From them you can create individual products for each customer and raise productivity and profitability.

Companies hire data engineers and machine learning engineers for exactly this. Find a piece of diamond, he says, and it rescues you from danger and helps you design your future.

01:04:14to 01:05:29Slide 7 & 8

KDD

KDD means Knowledge Discovery from Databases, a specific term he asks everyone to write down. The data sit in databases that you query with SQL, in systems such as PostgreSQL. Your task is to create knowledge from the data and then decide what to do, which is wisdom. The course builds a KDD process.

01:05:29to 01:08:15Slide 7 & 8

The KDD process, step by step

On the diagram, a green cylinder at the bottom left is the data and a red box at the upper right is the knowledge. In between, you select the data relevant to your purpose, preprocess it into tables and matrices, transform it, and extract the hidden patterns. A real person then interprets and evaluates the patterns and designs the future. The milk and diapers pairing was one such pattern.

  1. 01:05:371. SelectionFilter and extract target data relevant to business goal.
  2. 01:06:132. Pre-processingClean noise, resolve missing fields, remove duplicates.
  3. 01:06:453. TransformationReduce dimensions, normalize, encode matrices.
  4. 01:07:134. Data MiningApply algorithms (trees, clustering, association).
  5. 01:07:525. EvaluationInterpret patterns, validate utility, design future action.

Slide 8 emphasizes the cardinal law of KDD: Garbage In → Garbage Out (GIGO). Excellent algorithms cannot compensate for poor-quality input data.

Data types, categories, nominal and numerical data, and missing values.

01:19:51to 01:21:14Slide 9

Data types

He asks the class to take pens and write while he speaks. Integers, floats (real numbers, which are doubles), chars, strings, and date and time are all data. Bool has two options, true and false. These are familiar from Python and Java.

01:19:51 Slide 9 categorizes attributes into four essential data mining groups:

NumericalContinuous & discrete (Age = 21, Salary = £32k, Temp = 18.5°C)
Categorical / NominalDistinct names without order (Dept = CSEE, Colour = Blue, City = London)

The attribute data type directly dictates preprocessing strategy, visualization choices, and algorithm compatibility.

01:21:14to 01:24:15Slide 9

Categorized and uncategorized data

A customer form may ask for your horoscope sign from 12 options, or your eye colour from five. Those answers are categorized. Without categories the data set gets muddy, so prepare options and ask people to choose one. The same works for any area. Categorized data is better than uncategorized data. He checks for thumbs up.

Categorized

01:21:34 Choose one of a few options. A horoscope has 12, eye colour might have five. Body mass can be skinny, normal, fat, chubby or obese. Wealth can be rich, very rich or poor.

Uncategorized

01:22:19 Free text. Someone writes “bluish, then greenish, brown under the sun, black at night.” Everyone phrases it differently, and the data set gets confused.

01:24:15to 01:26:28Slide 9

Logical values, nominal and numerical

A logical value is a bool: true or false, one or zero. There are no grays, no halves and no real numbers. Gender or marital status can be asked this way. He corrects himself from three options to two.

Data can also be split into two kinds: numerical, which is numbers, and nominal, which relates to names, meaning strings and the opposite of numbers.

01:26:28to 01:27:08Slide 9 & 12

Missing data

When you collect data from individuals or customers, some fields come back empty: the horoscope, birth year, gender, marital status. There are techniques to replace missing values. He will show them later: “don’t worry.”
